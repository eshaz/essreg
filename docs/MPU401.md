# The ES1869's MPU-401

The ES1869 has an MPU-401 UART for MIDI in and out. This page collects what
is known about it on the Compaq Armada 1500c, what ESS's drivers and Linux's
es18xx do with it, and how essprobe's `mpu` cases measure what a Linux
driver still needs: whether MIDI out leaves the machine.

## The registers

* **The base.** LDN 1 registers 64h (bits 11:8) and 65h (bits 7:2) hold the
  MPU-401's base, and 0 turns it off (DS p.33). With card register 25h bit 0
  set, the MPU-401 is a device of its own, LDN 3, with its own IRQ; clear,
  it is part of LDN 1 and shares the audio interrupt (DS p.31).
* **The ports.** The base is the data port, and base+1 the command and
  status port. Status bit 7 is 0 while a byte waits to be read, a MIDI byte
  or an acknowledge, and bit 6 is 0 while the output takes another byte.
  Each way has an 8-byte FIFO (DS p.17-18), and a byte takes 320 us on the
  wire, MIDI's 31,250 baud.
* **The commands.** FFh resets the UART to Smart mode and answers FEh only
  if it was in Smart mode already; 3Fh goes to UART mode and answers FEh
  from Smart mode (DS p.42).
* **The interrupt.** The request is the input's read-data-available flag.
  ANDed with mixer 64h bit 6, it shows in Config_Base+6 bit 3 (DS p.39,
  p.62); Config_Base+7 bit 3 lets it onto the pin, and card register 28h
  names the IRQ (DS p.31). On a shared line the request has to drop before
  another source's can give a new edge, so a handler reads the bytes until
  status bit 7 is 1.

## On the laptop

* **As the BIOS leaves it** (sb16test's `chip.config` and the Linux report):
  card 25h = 80h, so part of LDN 1 on the audio interrupt, 27h = 28h = 05h,
  Config_Base 250h with +5 = 01h and +7 = 0Fh, and the MPU-401 at 330h,
  which answers FFh and 3Fh with FEh.
* **Input works.** Under Linux a MIDISPORT 2x2 sent 4,704 bytes into the
  port, and they arrived correct, on 330h and IRQ 5 (the Linux report).
* **The transmitter runs** at 321 us a byte, but no byte has been seen to
  leave through the harness on the docking connector. No register turns MIDI
  out on, in the data sheet, `ESSMPU.DRV` or the VxD, and the BIOS reads the
  machine as undocked, a possible gate that nobody has tested (the Linux
  report).

## What ESS's drivers do

* **ES1869.VXD**, unless "Do Not Want ES689" is set, sends FFh, 3Fh and the
  SysEx F0 00 00 7B 07 F7 to the MPU-401 at start, for an ES689 wavetable
  (o5:20AE), and clears mixer 64h bit 6 while a DOS program has the DSP,
  unless "DOS MPU-401 Interrupts" is set
  ([DRIVER_CONFIG.md](DRIVER_CONFIG.md#1-es1869vxd-settings)).
* **ES1869.DRV** registers ESMPUISR with the VxD when the MPU-401 has a port
  and no IRQ of its own, and serves its interrupts from the audio interrupt
  (3:4C23, callback 3:4EE6).
* **ESSMPU.DRV**, in `driver/`, is ESS's Windows driver for MIDI in and out.
  MIDI out through it under Windows 98, with a loopback cable or a second
  MIDI interface, settles whether the hardware sends.

## What es18xx does

Linux's ALSA driver, at the commit that
[ESS_EXTENDED.md](ESS_EXTENDED.md#sources) names:
* it finds the control port from mixer 40h's third and fourth reads, and
  writes LDN 1 64h and 65h and card register 28h only with a valid
  `mpu_port`;
* it writes mixer 64h = 42h, which turns the MPU-401's request on and also
  clears bit 0;
* its handler reads Config_Base+6 and calls the MPU-401's on bit 3;
* it creates the MPU-401 with `irq` -1 and `MPU401_INFO_IRQ_HOOK`, so input
  has no timer to fall back on when the handler misses bit 3;
* on ISA PnP it takes `mpu_port` from resource index 2, and its table lists
  ESS1869 and ESS1879 only, while ESS's drivers know Compaq's devices as
  CPQB023, CPQB0AB, CPQB0AC and CPQB0AD
  ([DRIVER_CONFIG.md](DRIVER_CONFIG.md#22-configuration-values)), so on the
  laptop es18xx needs the port given by hand.

## essprobe's mpu cases

The cases use the MPU-401 only where the BIOS put it, never turn it on, and
reset it to Smart mode at each case's start and end. What they change, card
28h, mixer 64h and Config_Base+7, goes to the restore list and back
([ESSPROBE.md](ESSPROBE.md)).

* `mpu.cfg`: Config_Base, card 25h, 27h and 28h, LDN 1 30h, 64h, 65h and
  70h, mixer 64h, Config_Base+5 to +7, and the status port, each with what
  it means.
* `mpu.reset` and `mpu.uart`: how long FFh and 3Fh take to answer FEh, and
  in how many status reads, against the 10,000 that Linux's driver allows.
* `mpu.irq`: Linux's settings, 28h at the audio IRQ, mixer 64h bit 6 and
  Config_Base+7 bit 3 on, and FFh's acknowledge as the request: whether it
  interrupts, Config_Base+6 at the interrupt, and after the handler read the
  byte.
* `mpu.irq_64off` and `mpu.irq_7off`: the same with each mask off: no
  interrupt, and Config_Base+6 bit 3 clear with 64h bit 6 off but set with
  Config_Base+7 bit 3 off.
* `mpu.loop`: in UART mode, a note on and off on channel 16 with running
  status, then a 64-byte SysEx (F0 7D 00 .. 3F F7), 77 bytes out, read back
  through a cable from MIDI out to MIDI in as they come. It reports what
  came back, the first byte's delay and the time a byte, and how long the
  output took. "Nothing came back" is INFO: no cable, or MIDI out doesn't
  leave the machine.
* `mpu.loopi`: the same read by the interrupt handler with Linux's settings,
  with the interrupts it took for the bytes.

## Checking MIDI out

1. Plug a MIDI cable from the harness's MIDI out to its MIDI in, start DOS,
   and run `ESSPROBE /C:mpu /O:MPU.TXT`, or `ESSCHECK.BAT`, whose first step
   runs the same cases.
2. If `mpu.loop` passes, MIDI out leaves the machine, and a Linux driver
   that sends nothing differs from what essprobe does.
3. If nothing came back while input works, MIDI out doesn't reach the
   harness: the dock's gate, or the harness's out line. A second interface
   then tells which: the MOTU PC-MIDI Flyer on the parallel port, under
   Windows 98 with MOTU's driver, takes the harness's MIDI out on its In A.
4. Send the reports back. For the Linux session, also send
   `/sys/bus/pnp/devices/*/id` and `resources`, `/proc/ioports`,
   `/proc/interrupts`, the driver's lines in `dmesg`, `amidi -l`, and what
   `amidi -p hw:0,0 -S 'F0 7D 01 02 03 F7' -d` gives with the cable in.
